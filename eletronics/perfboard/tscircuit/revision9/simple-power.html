Revision 9 — minimal power board on stripboard

Candidate, not released. A deliberately simple replacement for the revision 8 power board: one 12 V input, two buck modules and one switched, current-limited student rail. It gives up the voltage guards, the precise 12 mA trip and the fault latch (see below). Generated drawings and checks are not bench evidence. Build status: schematic checks pass, stripboard connectivity pass.

Schematic

Drawn with tscircuit from the same part list as the stripboard layout; a script checks that every net matches the layout, pin by pin. Series connections are wires; other pins end in a net label.

How it works

  • 12V_IN → F1 → D1 → 12V_SYS feeds two buck modules (5 V and 3.3 V). Each rail has a status LED.
  • Q1 (P-channel) switches 5V_SYS to the student rail. R_GP pulls its gate high, so the rail is off by default and with no power on the router.
  • Q2 and Q3 are in series: both need a high signal (EDU_PWR_EN and ROUTER_ALIVE) to pull the gate low. Pull-downs keep both off if a wire is lost.
  • R_S (470 Ω) limits a short on the student rail to at most 11.2 mA at 5.25 V. F_EDU is the backup. R_DT/R_DB/C_SENSE give the ESP32 half the rail voltage to measure.

Build order and checks

  1. Cut the strips first. Cut the 7 strips listed below with a 3 mm drill bit (a few turns by hand). Clear the copper dust and look through each cut against light.
    Check: Continuity: every cut must read open (no beep) between the two neighbouring holes. Check the 3 cuts next to J_OUT twice.
  2. Input stage. Solder J_IN, fuse holder F1 (fuse not fitted yet), D1 (band toward row 15), C2, C1 (+ toward row 3) and link L3.
    Check: Fit the fuse. Bench supply at 12 V, limit 200 mA: row 3 (12V_SYS) reads about 11.5–11.8 V. Reverse the supply: no current flows.
  3. Buck modules. Before connecting, set the 5 V module to 5.00 V and the 3.3 V module to 3.30 V with no load. Check each module for a 0 Ω connection between IN- and OUT-, then solder its three leads (rows and columns in the pin table). Add R_LED5, LED5, R_LED3, LED3.
    Check: Row 4 (5V_SYS) reads 5.00 V and row 2 (3V3_SYS) 3.30 V. Both LEDs light. Never exceed 5.05 V on row 4.
  4. Student-rail switch. Solder Q1, R_GP, Q2, Q3, R_PD_EN, R_PD_RA and links L1 and L2.
    Check: EDU_PWR_EN and ROUTER_ALIVE open: row 11 (5V_EDU) reads 0 V. Tie both rows to 3V3_SYS with a clip lead: row 11 rises to about 5 V. Remove either lead: it falls to 0 V.
  5. Current limit and monitor. Solder R_S, F_EDU, R_DT, R_DB and C_SENSE.
    Check: With the rail ON, put a milliammeter between row 11 and ground: it reads 10–11.5 mA (never above 12 mA). The monitor row 12 reads half of row 11.
  6. Interface header. Pull header pins 5, 6 and 8 out of the plastic, then solder J_OUT.
    Check: Continuity from each cable pin to its strip; pins 5, 6 and 8 have no pin. No neighbour shorts.

What you give up

  • Over-voltage and under-voltage guards (LTC4365): nothing cuts a failed buck, so measure the module before connecting.
  • Precise 12 mA trip and fault latch: replaced by the 470 Ω series resistor (short-circuit current at most 11.2 mA at 5.25 V) with the PTC as backup.
  • Fault output and CURRENT_SENSE: the ESP32 reads EDU_SENSE and switches EDU_PWR_EN off in software.
  • No hardware ceiling on 5V_SYS: the student rail can only rise to the buck setting. Set it to 5.00 V.

Open issues

  • 2N7000 gate threshold (up to 3 V) is close to the 3.3 V enable signal: bench-check that both transistors turn on, or swap in a logic-level n-channel part.
  • Voltage sag: at 2 mA the student rail drops about 1 V through R_S. Confirm the student circuits can live with that.
  • Fuse holder: a 5×20 mm holder is about 22.5 mm between pins and the holes give 22.86 mm (9 holes). Check the actual holder.
  • Buck modules must have a common negative (IN- joined to OUT-). Measure it. A module with isolated outputs needs a fourth lead.
  • Q1 body diode: a voltage on the student rail above 5V_SYS can feed back through the diode. Do not power student circuits from outside.
  • C1 (10 mm body) may need to lie flat or sit off the board; check the clearance next to the buck leads.
  • Joints, cuts and links are counted from the generated model. Build time is an estimate, not a measurement. No bench test has been done.
