Revision 9 — minimal power board on stripboard

Candidate, not released. A deliberately simple replacement for the revision 8 power board: one 12 V input, two buck modules and one switched, current-limited student rail. It gives up the voltage guards, the precise 12 mA trip and the fault latch (see below). Generated drawings and checks are not bench evidence. Build status: schematic checks pass, stripboard logic check pass; copper map estimated from photo, not measured.

How it works

  • 12V_IN → F1 → D1 → 12V_SYS feeds the blue XL4015 for 5 V and the red HW-155/LM2596 for 3.3 V. Each converter uses one common-negative pigtail to GND, after confirming IN− and OUT− continuity on that exact module. The converters’ onboard indicators remain on their modules; no extra rail LEDs are fitted on the stripboard. Verify the voltage and current capability of the exact modules before attaching loads.
  • Q1 (P-channel) switches 5V_SYS to the student rail. R_GP pulls its gate high, so the rail is off by default and with no power on the router.
  • Q2 and Q3 are in series: both need a high signal (EDU_PWR_EN and ROUTER_ALIVE) to pull the gate low. Pull-downs keep both off if a wire is lost.
  • R_S (470 Ω) limits a short on the student rail to at most 11.2 mA at 5.25 V. The MF-R050 PTC is omitted for now. R_DT/R_DB/C_SENSE give the ESP32 half the rail voltage to measure.

Stripboard

36 holes long × 32 holes wide (about 79 × 89 mm of holes; use a board a little larger). The 36-hole direction follows the portrait long axis, matching the supplied photo. Hole positions are written as row, column counted from the top-left of the top view. There are no planned cuts. Coloured lines are insulated underside links; the continuous copper buses carry shared nets directly. Dashed red rings on J_OUT are pulled pins. Buck modules remain off-board and use three pigtails each (IN+, common GND, OUT+), after confirming common negative on each exact module. Verify terminal markings and placement.

Physical copper pattern: the connectivity model uses the photographed long buses and short isolated island banks. Lane positions were estimated from the photo, not measured. Confirm the copper groups with a meter before soldering. Bus lanes replace repeated point-to-point connections for shared nets; the GND bars on columns 10 and 26 are joined by a link.

Input jack guide: 12 V center-positive barrel jack, female pigtail is shown below the board at the input corner. It is an off-board pigtail socket, not a board footprint. Connect its center-positive lead to J_IN 12V (row 36, col 2) and sleeve/negative lead to J_IN GND (row 36, col 3). Tie down the socket body/cable independently; do not rely on stripboard pads for plug insertion force. Verify the socket lead polarity with a meter before connecting the adapter.

Build order and checks

  1. Map the copper with a meter. This portrait drawing follows the supplied board orientation: 36 holes along its long axis × 32 across. It models longitudinal buses and separate short island banks inferred from the underside photo. No cuts are specified; verify every copper lane with a meter before soldering.
    Check: Use continuity mode to confirm the long buses and the short isolated islands match the drawn underside. If the lane pattern differs, stop and remap before fitting parts.
  2. Input stage. Keep the female barrel socket off-board and secure it mechanically. With the board disconnected, power the adapter and use a meter to verify about +12 V at the center relative to the sleeve; unplug it before wiring. Connect the socket center (+) by an insulated pigtail to J_IN 12V and sleeve (−) to J_IN GND. Solder J_IN, F1 with its 2 A fuse, D1 (band on the 12V_SYS side), C1/C2 and the listed insulated jumpers for the input net.
    Check: Use a current-limited 12 V bench supply for first board power-up, limit 200 mA: the D1 cathode / 12V_SYS landing reads about 11.5–11.8 V. The pictured adapter is rated 12 V DC, 3 A; the board input fuse remains 2 A.
  3. Buck modules. The drawing shows three landing holes per converter; mount each module off-board. The blue XL4015 is assigned to 5 V; the red HW-155/LM2596 is assigned to 3.3 V. Before wiring, identify IN+, IN−, OUT+ and OUT− from the board labels, then confirm IN− and OUT− are common with a meter. Set the blue module to 5.00 V and red module to 3.30 V with no load. Run three insulated wires from each module: IN+, common GND, and OUT+.
    Check: Measure the 5V_SYS landing at 5.00 V and the 3V3_SYS landing at 3.30 V. The converters have onboard indicators; they are not separate stripboard parts. Never exceed 5.05 V on 5V_SYS.
  4. Student-rail switch. Solder Q1, R_GP, Q2, Q3, R_PD_EN and R_PD_RA; install every jumper listed for these nets.
    Check: EDU_PWR_EN and ROUTER_ALIVE open: the 5V_EDU output pad reads 0 V. Tie both input signals to 3V3_SYS with clip leads: the 5V_EDU pad rises to about 5 V. Remove either lead: it falls to 0 V.
  5. Current limit and monitor. Solder R_S, R_DT, R_DB and C_SENSE. Follow the 5V_EDU jumper entries; MF-R050 is omitted.
    Check: With the rail ON, put a milliammeter between 5V_EDU and GND: it reads 10–11.5 mA (never above 12 mA). The EDU_SENSE pad reads about half of 5V_EDU.
  6. Interface header. Pull header pins 5, 6 and 8 out of the plastic, then solder J_OUT.
    Check: Continuity from each cable pin to its pad; pins 5, 6 and 8 have no pin. No neighbour shorts.

What you give up

  • Over-voltage and under-voltage guards (LTC4365): nothing cuts a failed buck, so measure the module before connecting.
  • Precise 12 mA trip and fault latch: replaced by the 470 Ω series resistor (short-circuit current at most 11.2 mA at 5.25 V). No PTC backup is installed in this version.
  • Fault output and CURRENT_SENSE: the ESP32 reads EDU_SENSE and switches EDU_PWR_EN off in software.
  • No hardware ceiling on 5V_SYS: the student rail can only rise to the buck setting. Set it to 5.00 V.

Open issues

  • 2N7000 gate threshold (up to 3 V) is close to the 3.3 V enable signal: bench-check that both transistors turn on, or swap in a logic-level n-channel part.
  • Voltage sag: at 2 mA the student rail drops about 1 V through R_S. Confirm the student circuits can live with that.
  • Fuse holder: a 5×20 mm holder is about 22.5 mm between pins and the holes give 22.86 mm (9 holes). Check the actual holder.
  • Each buck uses three board connections: IN+, common GND and OUT+. Use one module negative lead for input and output only after a meter confirms IN− and OUT− are common on that exact module. Verify terminal labels; dimensions and terminal positions are not measured here.
  • Q1 body diode: a voltage on the student rail above 5V_SYS can feed back through the diode. Do not power student circuits from outside.
  • C1 (10 mm body) may need to lie flat or sit off the board; check the clearance next to the buck leads.
  • Joints, cuts and links are counted from the generated model. Build time is an estimate, not a measurement. No bench test has been done.
